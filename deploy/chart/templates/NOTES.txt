Signing in is required: the console binds loopback and the Service reaches the
proxy, so its own port is in no Service at all. Everyone who signs in sees
everything the ServiceAccount can read -- authentication is not authorization,
and kubewhy has no per-user authorization model. Narrow what anyone can see by
narrowing the ClusterRole, and narrow who may sign in with ui.auth.emailDomains
or an --allowed-group in ui.auth.extraArgs.

This chart still ships no Ingress. If you put one in front, it must terminate
at the proxy's port ({{ .Values.ui.auth.port }}), never the console's.

The browser UI is running, and it has no authentication. Reach it deliberately:

  kubectl port-forward -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} svc/{{ .Release.Name }}-ui {{ .Values.ui.port }}:{{ .Values.ui.port }}
  open http://127.0.0.1:{{ .Values.ui.port }}

This chart ships no Ingress on purpose. The page renders cluster state and pod
logs, and there is no token to set -- anyone who can reach the Service sees
everything the ServiceAccount can read. Set ui.auth.enabled=true to put an
authenticating proxy in front of it.
